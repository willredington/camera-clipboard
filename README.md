# Camera clipboard: printed hardware (rev 3)

The capture, auto-OCR and document-portal software for the Pi lives in [`software/`](software/README.md).

Spring clip on the top edge like a real clipboard; camera boom on the LEFT edge (right-handed writer).
Pi Zero 2 W lives in the camera head; USB-C power comes in at the mast base and a 5 V pair runs up the hollow boom.
Board top surface = paper plane. Units mm. Everything is generated by `make_clipboard.py`
(`pip install trimesh manifold3d numpy rtree`, then `python3 make_clipboard.py out`), which also checks that the wire path is open
and that the clip lever doesn't collide with its base. Left-handed? Set `MAST_SIDE = 'right'` and regenerate.

## Blender
```
python3 render_assembly.py out            # writes out/assembly/*.stl + assembly.json (also a quick matplotlib preview)
blender --background --python blender_assemble.py -- out/assembly --render shot.png --save clipboard.blend
```
Or open Blender, Scripting tab, open `blender_assemble.py`, set `ASSEMBLY_DIR` at the top to the `assembly` folder, Run (use an empty file).
Every part is its own object, grouped into collections (Printed parts / Hardware / Electronics / Board and paper); 1 unit = 1 mm.
Tested headless with bpy 5.2 (Blender 3.x also supported by the importer fallback).

## You supply
| Item | Spec |
|---|---|
| Backing board | 260 x 330 x 3 mm hardboard / acrylic / plywood (US Letter: 22 mm side margins, 25 mm top/bottom) |
| Boom tubes | **Hollow 10 mm OD x 8 mm ID** carbon or aluminium. Blind-ended sockets, so **cut to length: vertical 204 mm, horizontal 91 mm** (+/-1 mm; `make_clipboard.py` prints the exact values) |
| Clip hardware | 1 x M3 threaded rod or bolt **~104 mm** with 2 nuts (pivot pin); **2 compression springs**, 6 mm OD, ~14 mm free length (a lever-clip pair from a hardware store works); thin foam or rubber strip for the jaw foot |
| SBC / camera | Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W (4 x M2.5 x 5 self-tapping into the standoffs); Pi Camera Module 3 **Wide** (4 x M2 x 5); Pi Zero camera cable, 22-pin to 15-pin, **150 mm** (the camera's own cable is the wrong size for a Zero) |
| Power in | Adafruit USB-C breakout, downstream (product 4090: 20.4 x 14.2 mm, 5.1 k CC pull-downs; use only VBUS and GND, no header), glued into the mast-base pocket with the port against the slot; 5 V / 2.5 A+ USB-C adapter (e.g. the official Raspberry Pi 27 W USB-C supply, which gives 5.1 V) |
| Cable | 24 AWG red/black pair ~0.7 m; 1000 uF 10 V cap across 5 V/GND at the head |
| Light | 8 mm 5 V COB LED strip ~250 mm in the underside groove, switched by a logic-level MOSFET from a GPIO |
| Screws | M4 x 16 (x 2 clip base, x 2 mast base, x 2 elbow, x 1 mast socket, x 1 head), M3 x 12 self-tapping (x 4 corners) |

## Clip: how it works
Pivot rod runs through both base ears and the lever's bore. The two springs sit in seats on raised bosses on the base and press the rear (thumb) end up, so the
front jaw clamps the paper top edge ~10 mm in from the edge. Press the thumb end down to open. Closed spring length (seat floor to dimple ceiling)
is ~11.4 mm, i.e. ~2.6 mm preload on a 14 mm spring; opening the jaw 8 mm takes ~31.5 deg of lever travel and compresses the springs to ~6.2 mm,
so check your spring's solid height is under ~5.5 mm. The lever dimples are pre-tilted 16 deg so the spring ends are never more than 16 deg out of
square. `make_clipboard.py` sweeps the lever through the full stroke and checks all of this (`CLIP_SEAT_BOSS`, `CLIP_DIMPLE_TILT`, `CLIP_SPRING_FREE`).

## Wiring
1. Solder the red/black pair to the USB-C breakout 5 V / GND, drop it into the mast-base pocket (port faces out through the slot).
2. Thread the pair up the vertical tube, through the elbow cavity, and along the horizontal tube (fish with a string before final assembly).
3. At the head, route out of the notch, along the groove across the plate, and solder to the Pi Zero 2 W header pads: **pin 2 (5 V)** and **pin 6 (GND)**. Add the 1000 uF cap.
4. Feeding 5 V through the header bypasses the Pi's input protection: use a clean regulated 5 V supply.

## Assemble
1. Slide the board corners into the four corner holders; a screw through each top bites the board.
2. Clip base on the top edge (centred), M4 screws at its two ends onto the board. Fit lever, springs and pivot rod; nuts on both ends.
3. Mast base on the left edge, centred, M4 screws onto the board; vertical tube into its socket.
4. Elbow on the tube top; horizontal tube into the elbow; camera head on the horizontal tube's far end. Lock the set screws.
5. Pi on the four standoffs (SD card toward the board's bottom edge side, so GPIO pins 2 and 6 sit at the end where the wire groove comes out, header edge toward the camera); camera on top of the plate with the lens through the window and its ribbon connector edge toward the socket block (the lens sits on the head axis, so the hole pattern is offset toward the Pi; the sensor's long side then runs along the page's long side). Route the camera ribbon out past the block, then round to the Pi's camera connector at the end away from the SD card; LED strip in the underside groove.
6. Stick the four ArUco markers (`aruco_markers_18mm_300dpi.png`, 100% scale, DICT_4X4_50 ids 0-3) in the margins, flat on the board.

## Check on the first print
- 0.25 mm fit clearance per surface (`FIT`); screw holes are self-tapping pilots (`SCREW_PILOT = 5.6` for heat-set inserts).
- Pi Zero 2 W (RP-008358), Camera Module 3 Wide (RP-008155 + official STEP) and Adafruit 4090 dimensions are from the published drawings; if you substitute a different USB-C breakout, set `USBC_PCB` / `USBC_PORT_Z`.
- Boom is cantilevered; if it flexes with the Pi and camera on it, use thicker tube or shorten the horizontal tube.
- At 205 mm the Wide module (102 x 67 deg) sees ~270 mm across its short axis (board width 260) and ~500 mm along the long axis, ~0.1 mm/px; the focus range is 5 cm to infinity.
